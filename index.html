PRACTICAL 01 Field Research & User Empathy

Problem Identification & Field Survey

Investigating supermarket friction, cashier queues, and customer frustration across retail environments.

FIELD SURVEY DATA

Methodology Note: Team-conducted primary survey of 41 shoppers across 8 cities (Anand, Navsari, Rajkot, Surat, Bilimora, Vapi, Vadodara, and Mumbai). These figures represent project evidence and team research findings, not generalized national statistics.

51.2%
Most Frustrated by Billing Wait Time

More than half of surveyed shoppers named waiting for cashiers as their single most frustrating in-store shopping experience.

61.0%
Have Abandoned a Supermarket Queue

Nearly two-thirds admitted to leaving full baskets or abandoning purchases solely due to excessive checkout line lengths.

8 Cities
Diverse Retail Demographics

Gathered across tier-1 metro (Mumbai) and tier-2/3 Gujarat commercial centres (Surat, Vadodara, Rajkot, Anand, Navsari, Vapi, Bilimora).

Candidate Problems Considered

  • Manual Cashier Bottlenecks: Peak shopping hours overwhelm stationary billing counters.
  • Hidden Expense Shock: Shoppers lose track of spending until the final bill total is computed.
  • Damaged Barcode Delays: Cashiers frequently stop billing to manually enter worn-out item codes.
  • Bulky Physical Cart Clutter: Narrow checkout lanes blocked by queues of overflowing trolleys.
SELECTED PROBLEM

Supermarket Queue Congestion & Slow Billing

Checkout queues consume 20–40% of total supermarket visit duration. Cashiers must physically handle, scan, bag, and collect payment for every item sequentially, creating severe friction and lost store revenue.

HOW MIGHT WE STATEMENT
"How might we design a secure, user-friendly mobile self-checkout application that enables supermarket shoppers to scan items and pay digitally in real time while shopping, thereby reducing billing wait times to under 2 minutes while preventing store theft?"

PRACTICAL 02 & 03 Ideation & Concept Evaluation

SCAMPER Framework & Pugh Selection Matrix

Exploring innovative interventions through systematic brainstorming, group voting, and weighted criteria scoring.

SCAMPER Ideation Matrix

7 Innovation Vectors
Vector Ideation Trigger Applied SmartScan Go Concept
S — Substitute Substitute the fixed cashier terminal with customer hardware. Use the shopper's smartphone camera as an individual optical scanner gun, eliminating stationary checkout hardware.
C — Combine Combine multiple validation technologies for higher accuracy. Combine standard 1D/2D barcode scanning with lightweight on-device AI recognition for produce and packaged goods.
A — Adapt Adapt security verification models from other industries. Adapt airline boarding gate and metro transit QR token models into digital exit-verification passes.
M — Modify Modify how pricing and subtotals are communicated. Provide an instant real-time running subtotal with live tax breakdown as items enter the physical basket.
P — Put to Use Put existing consumer smartphones to another functional use. Repurpose personal mobile devices into dedicated self-service retail POS terminals without store hardware overhead.
E — Eliminate Eliminate the primary point of customer friction. Eliminate physical billing queues, cashier handling steps, and printed thermal paper receipts entirely.
R — Rearrange Rearrange the chronological sequence of item billing. Invert checkout: scan continuously while traversing aisles instead of dumping the entire cart at the exit counter.

PRACTICAL 04 & 05 System Architecture & Usability Heuristics

Concept Development & Ergonomic Evaluation

Translating the selected C2 concept into an intuitive 5-touchpoint user journey governed by Nielsen-Norman usability principles.

⚖️

Academic Transparency: Implemented Prototype vs. Proposed Vision

Currently Implemented in Front-End Demo
  • Interactive product catalogue with real-time category filtering.
  • Manual product-code lookup & catalog matching.
  • Dynamic cart state with live quantity controls and ₹ total updates.
  • Simulated digital checkout and itemized printable receipt.
  • Demonstration exit-verification pass with unique security token.
Proposed Production Architecture (Future Scope)
  • Hardware smartphone camera live 1D/2D barcode scanning.
  • Lightweight computer vision AI model for produce identification.
  • Real-time supermarket POS database inventory sync.
  • Live UPI / payment gateway transaction settlement.
  • Physical automated store turnstile / exit-gate hardware integration.

The 5 Core User Touchpoints

01

Store Entry & Session Start

Customer opens the web app upon entering the supermarket; session initializes with a fresh shopping basket.

02

Product Identification

Shopper selects items from the visual catalogue or enters the unique shelf product code manually.

03

Cart Review & Edit

Immediate feedback displays item count, unit cost, live running subtotal, and flexible quantity controls.

04

Checkout & Bill Finalization

Shopper reviews order summary, verifies 5% estimated tax, and completes simulated checkout without cashier intervention.

05

Exit Pass & Verification

Single-use digital verification token is presented to store staff or exit scanner to validate paid items.

Ergonomic & Usability Heuristic Compliance (Practical 5)

Nielsen-Norman Evaluation
#1

Visibility of System Status

Header displays live cart count badge; instant toast feedback notifies users whenever items are added, modified, or removed.

#2

Match Real World System

Familiar supermarket basket terminology, standard Indian Rupee (₹) formatting, and recognizable grocery categories.

#3

User Control & Freedom

Immediate "+" and "−" buttons to adjust quantities, one-click item removal, and instant "Start New Basket" session reset.

#4

Consistency & Standards

Consistent retail layout: left-side inventory catalogue and right-side sticky basket summary following standard e-commerce patterns.

#5

Error Prevention

Validation prevents negative quantities, checkout button is disabled on empty carts, and invalid product codes trigger clear warnings.

#6

Recognition over Recall

Product cards feature vibrant grocery iconography, category tags, clear unit prices, and distinct shelf codes.

#7

Aesthetic & Minimalist Design

Clean ivory and forest-green retail palette with generous whitespace, avoiding intrusive banners, ads, or cognitive clutter.

PRACTICAL 08 Risk & Viability Assessment

Multidimensional Feasibility & Risk Analysis

Rigorous evaluation of technical constraints, market readiness, unit economics, and operational security vectors.

⚙️

Technical Feasibility

  • Camera API Access: Modern mobile browsers support WebRTC/MediaDevices for barcode capture.
  • Edge AI Models: Lightweight quantized models (e.g., MobileNet) execute client-side at >25 FPS.
  • Offline Resiliency: IndexedDB / LocalStorage buffers cart state during intermittent supermarket Wi-Fi drops.
📈

Market Feasibility

  • UPI Ubiquity: High adoption of mobile payments in India removes payment onboarding friction.
  • High Smartphone Penetration: Over 82% of urban supermarket shoppers carry 4G/5G smartphones.
  • Supermarket Competition: Modern retail chains seek ways to cut billing counter space and expand selling area.
💰

Economic Feasibility

  • Low Hardware Overhead: Eliminates ₹2,50,000+ stationary POS terminals per checkout lane.
  • No Expensive Smart Carts: Avoids fragile RFID carts costing ₹35,000+ each with high maintenance.
  • Staff Optimization: Shifts staff from repetitive manual scanning to customer service and exit auditing.
⚠️

Implementation Constraints

  • Low Lighting / Aisle Glare: Poor grocery aisle lighting may impact camera barcode detection rates.
  • Loose Produce Weight: Unpackaged vegetables and fruits require aisle self-weigh barcode scale integration.
  • Device Battery Drain: Continuous video feed scanning can consume noticeable battery over extended shopping trips.

Risk Assessment & Mitigation Strategies

Operational Security Matrix
Identified Risk Severity Likelihood Engineering Mitigation Strategy
Damaged / Scratched Barcodes Medium High Provide prominent manual 4-digit product code fallback entry, complemented by aisle staff handheld scanners.
Store Network Dead Zones High Medium Implement client-side basket buffering; transactions synchronize whenever network connectivity is restored prior to checkout.
Unscanned Item Theft / Shoplifting Critical Medium Combine digital exit-pass spot checks, random audits, and future computer-vision weight discrepancy heuristics.
Price Discrepancy & Outdated Cache Medium Low Timestamped catalogue cache with mandatory delta checks during final bill calculation against the store inventory server.
Consumer Payment & Data Privacy High Low No client storage of banking or UPI credentials; prototype does not collect sensitive payment data; production will use encrypted bank gateways.

🌱

Environmental Sustainability

Traditional supermarket checkouts consume millions of kilometers of non-recyclable thermal paper receipt rolls annually, which contain toxic BPA/BPS coatings. SmartScan Go promotes sustainability through:

  • 100% Digital Receipts: Elimination of chemical thermal paper rolls.
  • Energy Reduction: Eliminates heavy stationary CRT/LCD cash register consoles running 14 hours daily.
  • Reduced Hardware Scrappage: No specialized smart cart electronics destined for electronic waste landfills.
